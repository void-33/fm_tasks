Context
You are a Data Scientist at a telecom company. The business is losing customers to churn and
wants to take action. You have been handed historical customer data and one week to deliver a
production-ready recommendation.
That means understanding the problem deeply, running experiments across multiple models,
evaluating your results honestly, and making a justified decision about what to ship. There is no
prescribed model. You decide and you must defend every decision with evidence from your
experiments.
This is not a tutorial. There are no step-by-step instructions. You are expected to make decisions,
justify them, and take ownership of your results. Bring your questions, your failures, and your findings
to the session.

Tasks
The assignment is structured into five tasks. Each task builds on the previous one. Do not skip
ahead.
Task 1 Understand the Problem First
Before writing any modelling code, you must understand what you are actually solving. This task
is about formulating the problem and understanding the data.
1.2.3.4.5.Formulate the ML problem formally. What is X (your feature space)? What is y (your
target variable)? What probability distribution naturally models the target, and what does
that tell you about the right loss function?
What assumptions are you making about the data-generating process? List at least
three. For each assumption, describe what would happen to your model if that
assumption were violated.
Identify sources of uncertainty in this dataset. Where is the data noisy, biased, or
incomplete? Be specific, name the columns and describe the issue.
Profile the distributions of MonthlyCharges, tenure, and TotalCharges. What shape does
each distribution follow? Are there impossible or suspicious values? Decide how to
handle them and justify every decision.
The dataset has approximately 27% churn. Build a naive baseline: a model that always
predicts the majority class. What accuracy does it achieve? Why is this accuracy
misleading and why is this model dangerous to rely on?
Task 2 Classification Experiment Who Will Churn?
This is an open experiment. Your goal is to find the best linear classifier for predicting churn. You
are not told which model to use. You decide, you experiment, and you justify your choice.
Candidates to explore: Logistic Regression, Ridge Classifier, SGD Classifier. You are not required to
use all of them but you must justify what you tried and why you chose what you chose.
1.2.3.For each model you try: train it, evaluate it on the validation set, and record your results
in a comparison table.
Evaluate using metrics appropriate for an imbalanced dataset. Think carefully before
reporting accuracy. Which metrics actually tell you whether the model is useful to the
business? Your comparison table must include: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1,
ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, and Log Loss.
Plot ROC and Precision-Recall curves for your best model. The retention team has a
limited budget: they can only call 200 customers per week. At what threshold would you
deploy your model given this constraint? Justify your answer.
4.5.Inspect the coefficients of your chosen model. Which features drive churn the most? Do
the signs and magnitudes make business sense? If any coefficient surprises you,
investigate why.
Compare Batch Gradient Descent (LogisticRegression) and Stochastic Gradient Descent
(SGDClassifier). Do they converge to the same solution? Which is faster? When would
you prefer one over the other?
Task 3 Regression Experiment How Long Will They Stay? What Are They Worth?
Binary churn prediction tells you who might leave. Regression tells you how long they will stay
and how much they are worth. This task asks you to build regression models for at least one of
the following targets.
Choose at least one regression target:
•
 (a) Tenure as survival time how many months until a customer churns
•
 (b) Predicted churn probability score using the output of your classification model as a
continuous target
•
 (c) Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) = MonthlyCharges × predicted tenure
1.2.3.4.5.This is again an open experiment. Candidates to explore: Linear Regression, Ridge,
Lasso, Elastic Net. Try at least two. Compare results in a table and justify your final
choice.
Evaluate using MAE, RMSE, and R². Do not just report the numbers and interpret each
one. What does an R² of 0.55 actually mean in this context?
Plot residuals against predicted values. Is there a pattern? What does that tell you about
model fit or assumption violations?
Apply Ridge, Lasso, and Elastic Net regularization. Compare coefficients across different
regularization strengths. Plot the Lasso regularization path. Which features survive at
high regularization? Explain geometrically why L1 produces sparse solutions and L2
does not.
What does CLV enable the business to do that binary churn prediction alone cannot?
Task 4 Evaluation Integrity
A model with inflated metrics is worse than a model with honest ones. This task is about
verifying that your evaluation is trustworthy.
1.2.Split your data correctly into training, validation, and test sets. Justify your split strategy.
Check explicitly for leakage between splits.
Run k-fold cross-validation on your best classification model. How does CV performance
compare to holdout performance? Why might they differ?
3.4.Plot learning curves for your best model. Is it underfitting, overfitting, or
well-generalised? What is the correct intervention in each case?
Deliberately introduce a leakage feature into your pipeline. Observe what happens to
your metrics. Document the before and after. Explain why this is catastrophic in a
production system.
Task 5 Production Decision
You have run your experiments. Now you must commit to a decision and defend it.
1.2.Based on all your experiments, which classification model do you deploy? Which
regression model? Write a model card (approximately half a page) covering:
○ Chosen model and key hyperparameters
○ Key metrics on the held-out test set
○ Deployment threshold and justification
○ Known limitations of the model
○ What could go wrong in production
○ What you would monitor after deployment
Are linear models sufficient for this problem, or do you think a more complex model is
needed? What specific evidence from your experiments supports that view?
